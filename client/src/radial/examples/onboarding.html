Radial · onboarding

Four steps, a gate, and the door for people with nowhere to go

Each step gets its own visual grammar, because "how loud is someone" and "which platforms work" are not the same kind of question and one chart type would flatter neither. Four is the ceiling: each added step multiplies the skip rate, so a fifth should displace one of these rather than extend the run.

The flow

One frame, four screens

The gate asks the only question that matters — is there a BVC server for your world or not — and both answers lead somewhere. Step two moves players between channels on its own; step four's REC is a status readout on the ruler, not a control.

The sign-in code flow is deliberately unadvertised. There is no button for it. On the sign-in screen, type code@bvc.example.com into the address field to reach it — it is for a console player or a machine with no browser, and someone telling you to type that is the situation it exists for. The tab above is a reviewer's shortcut, not part of the product.

01 · Proximity

Walk up to someone.
You're already talking.

Voices get louder as players come toward you and fade as they leave. No lobbies, no invites, and nobody shouting over a call from three biomes away.

02 · Channels

Split off into
your own channel.

Running something across the whole map? Drop into a channel and everyone stays at full volume however far apart you get. Proximity keeps running underneath the entire time.

03 · Anywhere you play

It works with
the world you already have.

Your world stays exactly where it is. The mod goes on it; the BVC server runs on a machine you control.
