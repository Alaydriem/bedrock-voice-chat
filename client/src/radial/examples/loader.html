The mark writes on, the ring collapses inward, and then it waits

Six phases: the mark writes on flat in idle grey, takes its spectrum colours, starts dancing; the seventy-two ring bars fly inward onto their resting radius; the two beat together; the mark settles. What the sequence is saying is that the ring and the mark are one object — the relationship the rest of the product relies on, and one nobody has to be told if they watch it happen once.

One call, and an awaited finish

renderAt(t) is pure in t: the same timestamp always produces the same frame. That is what lets one implementation drive a live loop, the scrub bar below, a single-frame PNG and a WebM recording without any of them being a special case — and it is what makes the phase envelopes testable without a canvas.

Using it

In the app

Loader.mount The collapse is the completion signal, so a route change happens on a finished animation rather than on a timeout that may or may not have been long enough. 
import { Loader } from "$radial/core/intro/Loader";

const loader = Loader.mount(canvas);
await fetchServers();
await loader.finish();   // collapses to the flat colour row, ring still turning

// or declaratively
<Loader loading={busy} onfinished={() => goto("/dashboard")} />
What it replaces The current preloader: .bvc-ring (a borrowed CSS spinner), .bvc-badge, .bvc-shimmer, and the ten-second escape hatch bolted on when the spinner could not say whether anything was happening.
